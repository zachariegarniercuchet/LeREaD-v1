CITATION:  Certain Employees of STARBUCKS COFFEE CANADA, INC. v United Steel, Paper and Forestry, Rubber, Manufacturing, Energy, Allied Industrial and Service Workers International Union (United Steelworkers), Local 1-207, 2026 ALRB 5

 

January 16, 2026

 

Directed to:    Certain Employees of STARBUCKS COFFEE CANADA, INC. – Alena Leonova; McLennan Ross LLP – Timothy Mitchell; STARBUCKS COFFEE CANADA, INC. – Kate Fenton; Chivers Carpenter Lawyers – Drew R. Blaikie / Jacob Holloway; United Steel, Paper and Forestry, Rubber, Manufacturing, Energy, Allied Industrial and Service Workers International Union (United Steel Workers), Local 1-207 – Tara Cavanagh

 

RE:      An application for revocation of the bargaining rights of the United Steel, Paper and Forestry, Rubber, Manufacturing, Energy, Allied Industrial and Service Workers International Union (United Steelworkers), Local 1-207 brought by Certain Employees of STARBUCKS COFFEE CANADA, INC. affecting STARBUCKS COFFEE CANADA, INC. — Board File No. RV-01373

 

[1]           On June 2, 2025, certain employees of Starbucks Coffee Canada Inc. (the “Applicants”) applied to revoke the bargaining rights of United Steel, Paper and Forestry, Rubber, Manufacturing, Energy, Allied Industrial and Service Workers Internation Union (United Steelworkers), Local 1-207 (the “Union”) in relation to Board Certificate C2048-2022 covering “All employees at the Millrise Centre (Store #04619) in Calgary”. On June 11, the Board Officer issued a Board Officer’s report recommending dismissal of the revocation application on the basis it was untimely. Pursuant to section 52(3)(d) of the Labour Relations Code (the “Code”):

 

52(3)    An application for revocation of bargaining rights may be made by the employees in the unit

 

(d)        if a collective agreement for a term of more than 2 years is in force in respect of any of the employees in the unit, at any time

 

(ii)        in the 11th or 12th month of the 2nd or any subsequent year of the term, or

 

(ii)        in the 2 months immediately preceding the end of the term.

 

[2]           The Applicants are covered by a collective agreement with a term of August 4, 2023 to July 31, 2026. Thus, the “open period” for revocation applications set by section 52(3)(d) did not open until June 4, 2025.[1]

 

[3]           Upon receiving the Officer’s Report, the spokesperson for the Applicants wrote the Board. She stated:

 

I am writing to respectfully request that the Board exercise its discretion under Section 57 of the [Code] to waive the statutory bar period and permit resubmission of the employees’ Application for Revocation.

 

…

 

I respectfully submit that this minor technical filing error [the premature filing date] should not bar the employees from exercising their statutory right to apply for revocation. In support of this waiver request, I submit the following:

 

The application was submitted in good faith, and full compliance was made with all substantive requirements under the Code, including the required threshold of employee support. The premature filing resulted solely from a misunderstanding of the 10-month window calculation.

 

The filing was made only two days prior to the opening of the applicable window. This minor deviation resulted in no material prejudice or disruption to the Board’s processes or any party’s ability to respond.

 

…

 

I fully acknowledge responsibility for the technical filing error but respectfully submit that denying employees access to the decertification process based on a two-day early filing would be unduly harsh and inconsistent with the remedial intent of the Code. Granting consent to refile would promote industrial stability, employee choice, and labour relations fairness … Allowing the application to proceed would permit the employees to exercise their statutory right to vote on continued representation.

 

I therefore respectfully request that the Board consent to refile and allow the application to proceed in the interest of justice, fairness and employee choice.

 

[4]           Section 57(1)(b) of the Code states that if an application for revocation has been refused by the Board, withdrawn by the applicant, or remains before the Board without being actively pursued, the applicant may not, without consent of the Board, make the same or substantially the same application for 90 days. The section is worded in that manner to prevent parties from making a second, duplicative, revocation application (whether the original application has been dismissed, withdrawn, or simply sits in abeyance) until a certain time period has passed. However, it permits the party to seek the Board’s consent to file a second, new, duplicative application. A good discussion of the factors that the Board will consider in section 57 applications, albeit in the context of a certification, can be found in Telecommunications Workers Union (TWU), United Steelworkers Local Union 1944 v ADT Securities Services Canada, Inc., [2021] Alta. L.R.B.R. LD-055 at paras 22-28. A key consideration is whether permitting the early second application would create labour relations disruption.

 

[5]           The problem immediately apparent with the spokesperson’s letter is that it asks for two, quite contradictory, things. It references section 57 and speaks of “resubmission” of the application and “refiling”. However, it also refers to allowing the current application to proceed. In such circumstances, the Board Officer will reach out to the party, particularly a self-represented spokesperson for a group of revocation applicants, to clarify their options. The key options for the Applicants here would be to withdraw their June 2 application and seek consent of the Board to permit filing of a new, timely, application (which the Board notes would establish a new date for determination of the applicable Voters List); or they could insist that the current application continue. Here, it is very noteworthy that the application was never withdrawn. The Union made its position clear by letter dated June 13 that the Applicants’ section 57 request was improper given that the original application was not withdrawn and was continuing to be pursued by the Applicants.

 

[6]           In the face of the Applicants’ failure to withdraw the June 2 application, the Board followed its normal process to proceed to direct a vote with the ballot box sealed pending determination of the Applicants’ objection to the Board Officer’s recommendation of dismissal and other objections to the voting constituency. It is important for future applicants facing similar situations to understand the impact that insisting on the original application proceeding to a vote may have on the situation: the holding of a representation vote in the original application is one of the types of “labour relations disruption” that will be considered by the Board in considering consent under section 57 to file a second application.

 

[7]           When the current Vice Chair was assigned the hearing into this matter, the Vice Chair directed the matter be set down for the earliest available case management date. With self-represented litigants, a situation such as this calls out for clarification of the potentially contradictory positions of the party. Statements by the spokesperson for the Applicants in case management confirmed they have indeed insisted upon continuation of the initial application rather than any form of withdrawal of the initial application and filing of a new application.

 

[8]           In case management, the Board canvassed whether there were any disputed facts in relation to the Applicants’ request in its June 12, 2025 letter. All parties agreed that there was no disagreement with the facts contained within that letter, and the question of whether the Board could grant that request was purely one of law and applicable Board policy. On that basis, the Board determined the issue could be dealt with by written submissions alone. The Employer has made helpful submissions on the complex legal issues raised in this circumstance which might not otherwise have been canvassed by the self-represented Applicants.

 

[9]           The written submissions of the parties have come before a panel of the Board (Schick, Burns, Thibault).

 

The Applicants’ Submissions

 

[10]        The Board summarizes the Applicants’ submissions as follows:

 

•           The early filing was a minor procedural defect which ought not to bar the exercise of the employees’ ability to seek revocation;

•           “The timing irregularity was identified promptly, and I immediately sought the Board’s direction by emailing a letter on June 12, 2025, rather than withdrawing and refiling”;

•           The Board proceeded to direct a vote with full knowledge of the irregularity;

•           The Board should confirm that it has jurisdiction to proceed with a revocation application despite a technical defect in the application where it is technical and no prejudice results;

•           There would be no practical purpose in refusing to allow the employees’ votes on revocation to be considered at this time.

 

The Employer’s Submissions

 

[11]        The Board summarizes the Employer’s submissions as follows:

 

•           The Applicants’ request on June 12 should be framed as a request to “permit resubmission” of the revocation application, and therefore the question should be whether the Board grants that consent under section 57;

•           The Board has the authority to do so because the Applicants did not “actively pursue” the initial application;

•           The Code’s policy in preserving the democratic right to change bargaining agent through open periods is best met by permitting such consent; otherwise, the spokesperson’s error effectively closed the open period to the detriment of those rights;

•           The Board raised the legitimate expectations of the employees, by ordering the vote following the request for resubmission after having initially informed the employees through the posted Notice to Vote that the vote would only be ordered if the application met the Code’s requirements;

•           No prejudice has been caused to any party.

 

[12]        The Employer raises the case of Certain Employees of the Royal Canadian Legion Calgary v. Unite Here Canada Local 47, [2007] Alta. L.R.B.R. LD-064, in which the Board exercised its discretion under section 57 to consent to the filing of a second application for revocation in similar circumstances where the first revocation application was filed two days early. As the Employer acknowledges, that was a case where the applicants withdrew the initial application and were seeking consent to file a new application. The Employer states that is a distinction without a difference, as it argues in this case the Applicants simply chose not to “actively pursue” the initial application.

 

The Union’s Submissions

 

[13]        The Board summarizes the Union’s submissions as follows:

 

•           The Board has no authority under section 57(1)(b) of the Code to permit a revocation application to proceed that was filed outside the open period;

•           The Applicants have been clear that they are seeking the initial application to proceed. This was clear in the spokesperson’s letter of June 12, in her comments in case management, and in the Applicants’ written submissions;

•           The Applicants have only filed one revocation application and it is being “actively pursued”, in that it has not been withdrawn despite that option being made clear to the Applicants.

 

[14]        The Union refers to the case of IABSRI, Local 725 v Quolus Construction Services Ltd., [2019] Alta. L.R.B.R. 236 (“Quolus”) as support for the proposition that the first application remaining outstanding before the Board means it was being “actively pursued”.

 

Analysis

 

[15]        As outlined above, section 57 of the Code sets a limitation upon certification or revocation applicants bringing the same or substantially the same application without waiting the applicable 90 day period.  Previous versions of that section only limited second applications where the first was withdrawn or refused/dismissed. As can be seen in Miscellaneous Employees, Teamsters Local No. 987 v. Brandt Tractor Ltd., [2010] Alta. L.R.B.R. 56 (“Brandt”), this meant applicants could seek to avoid the time limit for filing a new application by not withdrawing the application and simply sitting on the application such that it was not refused/dismissed. As discussed in Brandt, the question then became one of the Board’s discretion whether to refuse the duplicative application as an abuse of process.

 

[16]        The Code was subsequently amended to expand the limitation on filing duplicative applications to situations where the initial application was refused by the Board, withdrawn by the applicant, or remained before the Board without being actively pursued. As seen in Quolos, applicants sought to avoid this limitation by arguing they were in fact actively pursuing the initial application. The Board found in Quolos that, while section 57 was indeed not applicable to this fact scenario, the circumstances were fundamentally different than Brandt and the Board dismissed the second duplicative application as an abuse of process. The Board’s view of duplicative proceedings was set out in paras. 14-17 of Quolos.

 

[17]        One of the fundamental rationales for the finding in Quolos is the ease with which the Code permits applicants to file a second application, through the express process of obtaining Board consent for the filing of a new application. As referenced above, the Board carefully takes into account practical labour relations considerations in deciding whether to grant consent for the filing.

 

[18]        In this case, seeking consent from the Board to refile a new revocation application that would be within the open period was the easy and obvious step.

 

[19]        Unfortunately, the Board is satisfied that the Applicants’ actions here did not take that step. While the June 12 letter contains reference to seeking consent for “resubmission” and “refiling”, the Board finds the Applicants’ actions were contrary to that interpretation of that letter. Failure to withdraw the original application, and thus having that application proceed to preliminary consideration of next steps by the original Board administrative panel, in the face of the Union’s letter of June 13, demonstrates the Applicants’ intention to pursue the alternative course referenced in their June 12 letter: seeking the Board to “allow the application to proceed”. This intention is confirmed by the spokesperson’s comments in case management and indeed by the Applicants’ written submissions (“… I immediately sought the Board’s direction by emailing a letter on June 12, 2025, rather than withdrawing and refiling” [emphasis added]). The Board finds this constitutes the Applicants “actively pursuing” the original application. In such circumstances, the Board would have no proper basis to consider the issue of section 57 consent, and indeed any new filing (had one been received) would likely have been an abuse of process without the first application having been withdrawn.

 

[20]        While it may have been preferable for the matter to have been set for early case management to clarify with the Applicants the issues with their June 12 request and the limited scope of the Board’s discretion under section 57, the Applicants were free to proceed down the path they chose. There may indeed have been strategic reasons for doing so: applicants for certification or revocation may choose their application dates carefully since, as referenced above, the voting constituency in a representation vote might change under the Board’s Voting Rules depending on the date of application, and a refiled second application would have a new application date.

 

[21]        In the face of the dispute between the parties, the Board followed its normal practice and directed a vote be held with the ballot box sealed pending resolution of all objections. The Board regularly directs votes in the face of disputes, including disputes on timeliness, and the holding of a vote cannot be viewed as creating legitimate expectations that a party’s position will be accepted and the vote counted.

 

[22]        As a result of the above, the Board is satisfied it has only the June 2, 2025 revocation application before it, and there is no basis to consider an application for consent under section 57. Further, contrary to the Applicants’ written submissions, there is no jurisdiction for the Board to permit a revocation application filed outside of the open period set in section 52(3)(d) of the Code to proceed, via section 57 or otherwise. The early filing cannot be characterized as a mere technical defect. The times set out for certifications and revocations in sections akin to section 52(3)(d) are mandatory: one can see this in the requirement in section 53(1)(a) that the Board shall satisfy itself that “the application is timely” before granting an application for revocation. Applications outside those permitted times are statute barred: see, for example, Brown & Marshall Electric Ltd. v IBEW, Local 424, [2001] Alta L.R.B.R. 492 at para. 35. Section 57 does nothing to permit the Board to alter that requirement, regardless of the procedural nature of the error or the good faith intentions of the Applicants.

 

[23]        Accordingly, the Board dismisses the request of the Applicants. As there is no basis to overturn the Board Officer’s finding that the application was untimely, the revocation application must be dismissed.
