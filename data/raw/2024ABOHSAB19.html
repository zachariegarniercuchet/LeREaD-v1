CITATION: Sherlock Environmental Services Ltd. v Occupational Health and Safety, 2024 ABOHSAB 19

 

November 7, 2024

 

Directed to:    Sherlock Environmental Services Ltd. - Megan Aldous; Emery Jamieson LLP - Natalie D. Tymchuk / Nura Awwad; Minister of Jobs, Economy and Trade c/o Occupational Health and Safety; Occupational Health and Safety - Erin Lane

 

RE:      An Appeal of an Order brought by Sherlock Environmental Services Ltd. affecting Occupational Health and Safety – Board File No. OHS-00117

 

[1]           On September 24, 2024, Sherlock Environmental Services Ltd. (the “Appellant”) commenced an appeal (the “Appeal”), under section 45(2)(a) of the Occupational Health and Safety Act, SA 2020, c O-2.2 (the “Act”) to vary a compliance order issued by Occupational Health and Safety of Alberta (“OHS” or the “Respondent”) on August 2, 2024.

 

[2]           The compliance order was issued under section 38 of the Act and related to compliance with section 20 of the Occupational Health and Safety Code, Alta Reg 191/2021 (the “OHS Code”), respecting airborne concentration methods to be used for harmful substances (the “Order”).

 

[3]           In it, the Appellant was ordered to:

 

1)    Submit at least two field blanks or 10% of the total samples, whichever is greater; and

 

2)    As air sampling volumes used had been lower than what the NIOSH (the National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health) 7400 procedure sets for clean environments (of 3,000 to 10,000 litres), proof of utilizing this proper methodology was to be provided.

 

[4]           The compliance date for the Order was set as August 16, 2024.

 

[5]           During this process, the Appellant requested feedback from OHS in August 2024 in regards to the requests under the Order. OHS did not respond to this request until September 11, 2024, at which time it confirmed the Order’s contents and extended the compliance date to September 25, 2024.

 

[6]           The Labour Relations Board (the “Board”) is the appeal body (the "Appeal Body") designated in section 45(1) of the Act. Pursuant to Rule 22 of the Board’s Rules of Procedure for OHS Appeals (the “OHS Rules”), a Vice-Chair (Semaine) was appointed, sitting alone, to address the OHS application to have the Appeal rejected summarily under section 45(5)(g) of the Act and Rule 31 of OHS Rules. Namely, OHS has requested that the Appeal Body reject the Appeal based on the Appeal not having been served on the Appeal Body within the set 30-day time period stipulated in section 45(3) of the Act. This is a decision on that preliminary matter.

 

[7]           After reviewing the Notice of Appeal, and the submissions of the Appellant and OHS on the timeliness issue, the Appeal Body concludes that the Appeal is not timely and summarily dismisses the Appeal on that basis. The Appeal Body's reasons follow.

 

Parties’ Positions

 

[8]           OHS submits that under section 45(3) of the Act an appeal must be commenced by serving on the appeal body a notice of appeal within 30 days of being issued the compliance order. It also notes that under Rule 31 of the OHS Rules, the appeal body may reject an appeal summarily, where it is without merit, frivolous, trivial, vexatious, filled with improper motives or otherwise an abuse of process. Examples of this are given under that rule, including under sub-part (a), which references the situation where an appeal was not served on the appeal body within the 30-day period referenced in section 45(3) of the Act. OHS submits that a statutory time limit cannot be extended.

 

[9]           In relation to above, OHS outlined that it issued its Order on August 2, 2024, and that the Notice of Appeal was served on September 24, 2024, more than 30 days later. OHS acknowledges that the deadline to comply with the Order was extended on September 11, 2024, but it submits this further Contact Report extending timelines did not contain a new order. Further, OHS argues that the extension portion is not being appealed; rather, it is the compliance requirements set out in the Order issued in August.

 

[10]        Meanwhile, the Appellant submits that it requested feedback on documents it provided in August, but OHS did not respond until September 11, 2024, which was past the 30-day time limit (based on the initial Order date). The Appellant submits it was prejudiced by attempting to work with OHS in good faith and in awaiting their feedback.

 

[11]        In response to OHS’s submissions, the Appellant further responds by arguing that the Appeal Body does have the authority to extend timelines and relies on the following in support of that argument:

 

  • Rule 63 of the OHS Rules, which allows the Appeal Body to relive against a non-compliance with the OHS Rules, in order to avoid a miscarriage of justice;

 

  • Section 46(9) of the Act, where the Appeal Body is granted the authority to establish rules of procedure respecting the hearing of appeals;

 

  • Section 46(11) of the Act, where the Appeal Body is granted exclusive jurisdiction to exercise powers conferred on to it by the Act; and

 

  • As section 12(1) of the Labour Relations Code, RSA 2000, c L-1 states that the Board shall perform its powers and duties in a manner consistent with the jurisdiction conferred upon it, the Board has been granted the exclusive jurisdiction to hear OHS appeals and develop and apply its own rules of procedure.

 

 

Decision

 

[12]        The Appeal Body rejects the Appellant’s Appeal summarily for failure to serve the Notice of Appeal within the 30-day period referenced in section 45(3) of the Act.

 

[13]        Section 45 of the Act governs the OHS appeal process and its relevant portions read:

 

45(1)  The Labour Relations Board under the Labour Relations Code is the appeal body for the purpose of this Act.

 

(2)  A person

 

(a)    to whom an order is issued under section 38, 39 or 40 or in relation to whom an order is varied by a Director under section 41,

 

…

 

may appeal the order, administrative penalty, cancellation or suspension, the findings in the record referred to in clause (d), the findings in the report referred to in clause (e) or the order referred to in clause (f) to the appeal body.

 

(3)   An appeal under subsection (2) must be commenced by serving a notice of the appeal on the appeal body within 30 days of being served or given notice of the order, administrative penalty, cancellation or suspension, record or report.

 

…

 

(5)  After considering the matter being appealed, the appeal body may by order

 

…

 

(g)    reject the matter summarily where the appeal body is of the opinion that the matter is without merit, or is frivolous, trivial, vexatious, commenced with improper motives or otherwise an abuse of process.

                       

…

 

[14]        The Appeal Body notes the Order was issued on August 2, 2024, thus, to meet the 30-day deadline under section 45(3), the Appellant needed to serve the Notice of the Appeal on the Appeal Body by September 2, 2024 and the Appellant did not take this step until September 24, 2024, well past this deadline.

 

[15]        As was recognised in Kehewin Cree Nation v Mulvey, 2013 ABCA 294 (at para 11), it is a longstanding principal that a time limit in a statute cannot be amended, either by shortening or extending, unless that governing statute expressly gives a body that power. The Appeal Body has not been granted this power. Indeed, as the Appeal Body recently held in Thorkelson v Shell Canada Limited, 2024 ABOHSAB 17, there is no authority in the Act to ignore or expand this 30-day period. Rather, the jurisdiction of this Appeal Body is confined to consider only appeals that have been properly commenced within that period (at para 9).

 

[16]        The Appeal Body recognizes that following the issuance of the Order, there were ongoing communications between the Appellant and OHS, and the deadline to comply with the Order was extended. Based on this, the Appellant argues, it follows that the timeline governing the appeal period is displaced by this compliance deadline extension.

 

[17]        However, section 45(3) of the Act is not so permissive. It states that an appeal must be commenced within 30 days of being served or given notice of the order, and it cannot be said that the Appellant did not have notice of the Order when it was issued on August 2nd from the site visit then and where it was served electronically that day. Further, when the timelines for compliance were extended, OHS did not reissue a new compliance order, and the underlying contents of the Order remained the same. Finally, on the Order itself, there is a warning at the end that the initiation of an appeal does not suspend the order unless a stay is granted and that requests for an appeal must be initiated within 30 days of the initial date of service (emphasis added). In all, the extension of the compliance date does not change when the Appellant was served or given notice of the Order.

 

[18]        The Appeal Body has considered the Appellant’s other arguments outlined above and does not accept them for the following reasons.

 

[19]        While Rule 63 of the OHS Rules allows the Appeal Body to relieve against non-compliance with the OHS Rules in order to avoid a miscarriage of justice, the Appellant is not just asking for relief from the OHS Rules, but rather from a time limit set in the statute itself, in section 45(3) of the Act. The Appellant also seeks to rely on section 46(9) of the Act, but this provision enables the Appeal Body to establish rules of procedure, which are embodied in the OHS Rules, and which are only procedural rules to give effect to the statutory authority given under the Act: they cannot override or otherwise conflict with the Act, and if they did, Rule 3 clearly provides the Act shall prevail. Section 46(11) of the Act is also argued, but this provision grants the Appeal Body exclusive jurisdiction “to exercise the powers conferred on it by or under this Act”, thus again tying the Appeal Body to powers it is granted under the Act. Finally, the Appellant cites section 12(1) of the Labour Relations Code, which reaffirms that the Board/Appeal Body shall perform its powers and duties in a manner consistent with the jurisdiction (emphasis added) conferred upon it by the Labour Relations Code or any other enactment (such as the Act) conferring jurisdiction on the Board.  In Ishdorj v Health Watch Medical Clinic Ltd., 2024 ABOSHAB 4, the Appeal Body rejected an argument that it had authority to order costs under section 12 of the Labour Relations Code, on the basis that its statutory authority must be located in the Act itself, which is the legislation that designates it as the Appeal Body (at para 32). 

 

[20]        For the reasons above, the Appeal Body finds the within Appeal was not commenced within the 30-day period stipulated in section 45(3) of the Act, and accordingly, under section 45(5)(g) of the Act and Rule 31(a) of the OHS Rules the Appellant’s Appeal is rejected summarily.

 

[21]        Finally, the Appeal Body notes the Appellant’s request in the underlying Appeal that identifying information about its clients, including site addresses and client names, not be published, including in the air monitoring reporting. While OHS does not oppose this request, the Appeal Body need not address this issue as a result of its preliminary ruling to dismiss the Appeal of the Order, and otherwise notes it was not necessary to reference that information in this decision.
