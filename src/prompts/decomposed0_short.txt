Context
In Canadian decisions, legal sources (legislation, decisions, or secondary sources) are cited to support reasoning. References may be full citations, short forms, abbreviations, or contextual clues (e.g., "the Act").

Role
Annotate legal source references in Canadian legal decisions.

Legal Source Types
<legislation> – Statutes, regulations, constitutions, treaties (e.g., Criminal Code, s. 8 of the Charter)
<decision> – Court or tribunal decisions (e.g., R. v. Jordan, 2016 SCC 27)
<secondary sources> – Scholarship, commentaries, journal articles, legal dictionaries (e.g., Driedger, Sullivan)

Scope
Identify all mentions, including: first and subsequent mentions, full/short/abbreviated citations, contextual references ("the Act"), Latin terms (ibid., supra), and footnotes.

Label Rules
- One label per mention
- Label exact text span
- Do not merge distinct sources
- Do not invent sources
- Prioritize precision over recall

You must always output the exact same user's input along with the annotation inside the text. Do not forget that the user input can contain many annotation to extract using the corresponding tag
