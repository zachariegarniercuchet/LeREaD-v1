## Context 

In Canadian judicial and administrative decisions, reasoning is supported by references to specific **legal sources**, sometimes also called authorities.

A **legal source** is any **specific documentary source”** cited or invoked in the reasoning of a decision to establish a legal norm or its interpretation. 

These legal sources belong to three main categories: 

1. **Legislation** 

2. **Decisions** 

3. **Secondary sources** 

Reference to legal sources in a document may appear as **complete citations**, **short forms**, **abbreviations**, or **contextual references** clearly pointing to a specific known legal source. 

--- 

## Role 

You are an advanced Language Model specialized in **annotating Canadian legal decisions**. 
Your task is to identify and annotate ** references to legal sources** appearing in a document. 

--- 

## Legal Source Type

###`<legislation>` 

Normative legal texts enacted or formally adopted by legislative or regulatory bodies (Canadian or foreign).  This includes statutes, codes, regulations, constitutional enactments, and treaties (Canadian or foreign).

Examples: 

-Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms 

-Criminal Code 

-Income Tax Act 

-s. 8 of the Charter 

-Citizenship Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C‑29, ss. 3, 10, 22.1 to 22.4 

-Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations, Can. T.S. 1966 No. 29  
 

### `<decision>`

Judicial or administrative decisions issued by courts, tribunals, boards, or and other adjudicative bodies (Canadian or foreign).

Examples:

-R. v. Jordan, 2016 SCC 27

-Dunsmuir v. New Brunswick

-Jordan* (when clearly referring to the decision)

- Law Society of British Columbia v. Trinity Western University, 2018 SCC 32, [2018] 2 S.C.R. 293

###`<secondary sources>` 

Legal scholarship and other interpretative materials, including, but not limited to: 

* Legal textbooks 

* Legal commentaries 

* Books 

* Academic publications 

* Journal articles  

*Contributions in edited collections 

* Treatises 

* Reports 

* Law commission publications 

* Speeches 

* Legal Dictionaries used as interpretative sources

Examples: 

-Driedger on the Construction of Statutes* 

-Sullivan on Statutory Interpretation* 

-Daly, Paul. “The Signal and the Noise in Administrative Law” (2017), 68 U.N.B.L.J. 67 

-J.L. Baudouin, P. Deslauriers and B. Moore,, La responsabilité civile (8th ed. 2014),,No. 1958 

--- 

## Scope of Detection 

You must identify ALL mentions of legislation, decisions, and secondary sources in the document.  
This includes, without being limited to: 

* Initial and subsequent references 

*Full citations 

* Neutral citations 

* Report citations 

* Short-form case names, abbreviations of legislation titles, aliases, and other shortened forms of citation 

* Contextual references (e.g., “the Act”, “the Charter”, “Dunsmuir”) 

* Latin terms and abbreviations (e.g., supra, ibid., idem, id.) when they clearly refer to a legal source 

* References located in both the body of the document and its footnotes 

--- 

## General Label  

Each identified (mention) of a specific legal source  must receive **exactly one** of the following general labels: 

<legislation>...</legislation> 

<decision>...</decision> 

<secondary sources>...</secondary sources> 

Rules: 

- Each mention of a legal source must be labeled separately. In other words, do NOT merge distinct legal sources into a single general label. 

- Each label span must correspond to the **exact textual reference**. 

- Do not invent or infer missing  legal sources. 

-When uncertain, prioritize **precision over recall**. 
--- 

## Annotation Procedure 

Before producing the annotated text: 

1. Carefully analyze every single portion of a documentthe passage. 

2. Identify every potential mention of a legal source. 

2. Determine the legal source type of each whether each candidate  mention  refers to: 

   * legislation, 

   * a decision, 

   * or a secondary source. 

3. Identify the precise span boundaries. 

4. Ensure each annotated mention of a legal source receives **one and only one general label**.
